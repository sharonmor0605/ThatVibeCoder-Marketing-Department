# ICP

Built by the lock-your-icp skill, one long interview at a time; a section that hasn't been through that interview yet stays a stub rather than a guess.

## Community Camp Families

### The One Liner
> "I help two-income working parents like Mandy go from feeling torn between a crying child and a Slack message to knowing her kid is safe, growing, and values-aligned at EDMO — so she can actually be present at work without the guilt."

### The One Person
**Mandy.** Lives in the Sacramento area. Two-working-parent household — she works from home, her husband works out in the world. Two young kids. Real anchor, not a pure hypothesis, though not yet a specific named family EDMO has directly served (built from the owner's read of who these households actually are).

The household type itself was tested, not assumed: the owner first guessed the split might be "career mom into STEAM" vs. "stay-at-home mom into SEL," checked with her CR Director, and got back a confirmed answer — the real pattern is two working parents, not two different household types. The existing "Working Wendy" persona (dual-income household) already had this right without anyone deciding it on purpose.

**Open, unconfirmed:** whether a STEAM/SEL split still exists *within* a two-working-parent household (e.g., between the two parents), or whether the real distinguishing factor is something else (schedule logistics, who handles enrollment). Also open: the owner's hunch that wealthier communities lean SEL and lower-middle-class communities lean STEAM — tested against real Community Camp family survey data (Sierra College/Rocklin vs. Casa di Mir Montessori/San Jose) and left **unconfirmed** — the sample was too thin (13 usable Rocklin responses, 3 usable San Jose responses) and the survey never asks the question directly. Recommendation, not yet acted on: add one direct question to next season's survey, tagged by site.

### Current State and What It Costs
Because Mandy works from home, she's physically present but still can't actually watch her kids and work at the same time — a different problem than the "needs full-time childcare" framing most marketing assumes. The real cost is emotional, not just logistical: when her younger child wants attention and she can't give it because she's mid-Slack-message, she feels genuinely torn. She can hear her kid crying and knows she can't go help. At the same time, she genuinely enjoys her work and wants to perform well at it. It's an identity conflict — good mom vs. good employee — not a scheduling inconvenience.

### Dream State
She knows her kid is in good hands: developing with age-appropriate enrichment, learning good character skills through the EDMO Method (the S.P.A.C.E. framework — Self-Awareness, Problem-Solving, Advocacy, Collaboration, Empathy), which lines up with her own personal values. With that confidence, she's able to actually be present for her work, which matters just as much to her as being present for her kids. The tension from the current state resolves on both sides at once.

### What Makes Them Click
Not stats — she never mentioned a number. What moves her:
- Seeing EDMO show up organically in her social media feed
- Hearing another mom talk about it directly
- Being impressed by the professionalism and communication in any interaction with the organization
- Feeling the safety, professionalism, and enthusiasm of EDMO staff firsthand, out in the field

Org-wide proof points (98% SEL growth, 81% homework completion, 96% return rate) exist and are real, but for Mandy specifically, trust comes from social proof and lived experience, not numbers.

### What Turns Them Off
Discovering EDMO is "just words" — saying the right things without delivering on them. Anything that feels corporate makes her feel like a number instead of someone special, which reads to her as a values mismatch. (No single concrete example was landed on for this one — left honest rather than padded.)

### Who They Are Not
A parent who isn't honest about their own child or themselves, who puts excessive expectations on EDMO, who doesn't take EDMO's professional recommendations seriously, or who treats staff like "the help." Notably, this mirrors the District Partnerships "who they're not" almost exactly (there: nickel-and-diming and disrespecting program cost/integrity) — across both audiences, the wrong fit is someone who won't treat EDMO as a professional partner.

### Their Words
> "As a mom, I really appreciated finding a camp that made hands-on STEAM learning and social-emotional growth so engaging."
— Real quote, Sierra College (Rocklin) family survey response, Summer 2026 Community Camp.
